Passive Compliance Control with Force Regulation

A passive impedance controller on the KUKA iiwa that keeps contact stable, regulates force and stays safe around people.

LabFigueroa Robotics Lab, University of Pennsylvania Year2024 – 2025 StackC++, Python, ROS, Gazebo

Problem

In contact-rich tasks such as insertion, the end-effector drags across a surface and force readings get noisy. A controller that reacts directly to that noise can oscillate: a push from the right makes the robot retract left, it hits the left wall of a tight channel, gets pushed back right, and so on.

Approach

  • A passive dynamical system that dissipates energy, with a damping matrix updated in real time. Controlling its eigenvalues regulates the applied force and keeps contact with the surface steady.
  • An energy tank that keeps the controller stable and safe for human-robot collaboration.
  • Stable dynamical systems learned from fewer than 20 demonstrations (LPV-DS), with modulation for accurate, convergent and obstacle-aware motion.
  • Camera perception and motion capture for real-time object localization and trajectory recording.
  • Simulated and tuned in ROS and Gazebo before running on the real robot.

Result

The KUKA LBR iiwa keeps compliant, stable contact while tracking a desired force, as shown in the videos, and stays safe during physical contact with people.
