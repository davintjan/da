Steerable Tape-Spring Needle for Autonomous Sharp Turns Through Tissue

Abstract

Steerable needles offer a minimally invasive method to deliver treatment to hard-to-reach tissue regions. We introduce a new class of tape-spring steerable needles capable of sharp turns ranging from 15 to 150 degrees with a turn radius as low as 3mm, which minimizes surrounding tissue damage. In this work, we derive and experimentally validate a geometric model for our steerable needle design. We evaluate both manual and robotic steering of the needle along a Dubins path in 7 kPa and 13 kPa tissue phantoms, simulating our target clinical application in healthy and unhealthy liver tissue. We conduct experiments to measure needle robustness to stiffness transitions between non-homogeneous tissues. We demonstrate progress towards clinical use with needle tip tracking via ultrasound imaging, navigation around anatomical obstacles, and integration with a robotic autonomous steering system.

Key Responsibilities
  • Developed a new class of tape-spring steerable needles capable of sharp turns (15°-150°) with a minimal turn radius of 3mm, reducing tissue damage in minimally invasive procedures
  • Derived and experimentally validated a geometric model for steerable needle design
  • Evaluated manual and robotic steering of the needle along Dubins paths in tissue phantoms simulating healthy and unhealthy liver tissues (7 kPa and 13 kPa stiffness)
  • Demonstrated needle tip tracking via ultrasound, navigation around anatomical obstacles, and integration with robotic autonomous steering systems

Development of Passive Compliance Controller with Force Regulation

Background

The main challenge in contact-rich tasks is ensuring that the controller maintains compliance. In tasks such as insertion, where the end-effector must drag across a surface, force sensor measurements can become noisy. This noise can lead to unstable robot control. For example, if an external force suddenly pushes the end-effector from the right, the robot may retract to the left. However, in a constrained environment like a tight channel, this retraction could cause the robot to collide with the left side, triggering another reaction force that pushes it back to the right. This cycle can result in uncontrolled oscillations.

To address this issue, a passive dynamical system is implemented to dissipate energy and maintain passivity. The system continuously updates its damping matrix in real time, adjusting compliance in response to external disturbances. By controlling the eigenvalues of the damping matrix, the robot can regulate the applied force and maintain a steady interaction with the surface.

Key Responsibilities:
  • Developed passive impedance control with force regulation on Kuka LBR IIWA robot for contact-rich tasks
  • Developed and simulated tasks in ROS using Gazebo to optimize robot performance before real-world deployment
  • Integrated kinematics and path-planning with camera-based object detection for versatile task execution
  • Implemented energy tank concept to ensure controller stability for human robot collaboration
  • Employed learning algorithm (LPVDS) in Matlab to create a stable dynamical system based on minimum amount of demonstration (less than 20)

Implementation of Compliance Controller and Imitation Learning for a 6 DOF Robot Arms for Building Bracket Installation

Background

This project consisted of two main tasks. The first was to migrate the force and compliance controller from the UR20 robot arms to the FR20 robot arms and ensure they performed as effectively as the UR20 robots. The second task was to develop an imitation learning model capable of automatically performing bracket insertion given an initial position and an estimated goal pose. The main challenge in the second task was that the perception system, which estimates the goal pose, introduced errors in orientation and translation. Therefore, the imitation learning model needed to compensate for these errors.

Key Responsibilities:
  • Implemented compliance and force control on a 6 DOF robot arm for tasks that require contacts and dragging such as channel search, bracket alignment, and nut tightening/loosening, improving installation precision
  • Tuned control gains (stiffness, PD, error scale), achieving a 95% success rate in bracket installation
  • Trained and developed an imitation learning model for automated bracket insertion, achieving position and orientation RMSE of < 0.0001 from expert trajectories
  • Automated pipeline for automatic bracket insertion data collection for model training purposes
  • Prepared both the software and hardware for robot operation, including launch files, controller gains, URDF, FT-sensor mount, pneumatics wiring, and microcontroller and PCB wiring

Soft Actuator & Variable Stiffness Link

Abstract

With the increasing demand for robots, their development has reached an all-time high. However, most robots currently being developed are rigid and constructed from hard materials. While this type of robot excels in precision and movement speed, it requires a complex control system to ensure smooth and accurate operation. The primary drawback of rigid robots is their inability to handle fragile objects safely. Additionally, rigid robots are often unsuitable for collaborative environments involving humans due to the hardness of their materials, which poses safety risks.

To address these limitations, a pneumatic-based soft robot using silicone as the primary material was developed. This type of soft actuator has previously been created by researchers at Harvard University as a grasping mechanism for hand rehabilitation. In this study, the design of the soft actuator is modified to enhance the force it can generate, enabling its use in actuating an elbow mechanism. Furthermore, a variable stiffness link is developed, allowing the stiffness of the soft robot system to be adjusted as needed.

The goal of this research is to produce a soft actuator capable of generating greater lifting force than its predecessors, making it suitable for moving a jig that simulates an elbow mechanism.

Key Responsibilities:
  • Design of soft actuator & Variable stiffness link that can generate 9N lifting force at 37 kPa
  • When stiffens, the system provides stiffness up to 3x compare to its relaxed state
  • These images illustrates the design, the actual soft actuator, and the jig used to test the lifting force of the actuator
